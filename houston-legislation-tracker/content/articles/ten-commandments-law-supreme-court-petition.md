This is an update to our explainer, [What Texas' Ten Commandments Law Actually Does](/articles/what-texas-ten-commandments-law-does).

## What changed?

On Monday, August 17, 2026, families challenging Texas' SB 10 filed a joint petition asking the U.S. Supreme Court to review the law. The petition covers two cases, *Nathan v. Alamo Heights ISD* and *Cribbs Ringer v. Comal ISD*. In April, the full Fifth Circuit ruled 9 to 8 against the families in both.

## What does the petition ask?

According to K-12 Dive's reporting, the families ask the justices to decide two questions: whether laws requiring Ten Commandments displays in classrooms violate the First Amendment's Establishment Clause, and whether they violate parents' rights under the Free Exercise Clause to direct their children's religious upbringing.

The families' lawyers argue that the Fifth Circuit's decision conflicts with the Supreme Court's 1980 ruling in *Stone v. Graham*. The Fifth Circuit's majority held that a later Supreme Court decision changed the legal approach. The state has defended the law and the ruling. Both sides' arguments are laid out in our [full explainer](/articles/what-texas-ten-commandments-law-does).

## A related Louisiana case

Texas is not the only state with such a law. According to the National Constitution Center, a three-judge Fifth Circuit panel had ruled against Louisiana's Ten Commandments law, but the full court set that ruling aside in February 2026, saying the challenge was premature. It decided the Texas case could be heard by the full court, which led to the April decision.

## What happens next?

The petition is a request, not a hearing. The side that won below can file a response opposing review. The justices then decide whether to take the case. If they do, the case would be argued in a future term. If they do not, the Fifth Circuit's ruling stands.

As of September 20, 2026, we did not find a filed response from Texas or a date on which the Court will consider the petition. We will update the [SB 10 tracker page](/legislation/ten-commandments-in-classrooms) when that changes.
