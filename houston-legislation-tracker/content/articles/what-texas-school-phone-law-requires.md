Before 2025, whether a Texas student could use a phone at school depended on the district. House Bill 1481 changed that. Every public school district and open-enrollment charter school must now have a written policy that prohibits students from using personal communication devices on school property during the school day.

## What does the legislation do?

HB 1481 amended Section 37.082 of the Texas Education Code. Its central requirement is a policy: each district and charter school must adopt one that bans student use of personal communication devices during the school day. According to the Statesman, such policies were optional before.

The definition of a device is broad. District guidance describes it as covering phones, tablets, smartwatches, earbuds, gaming devices, and any other device that can send or receive data. Devices issued by the school for instruction are not covered.

The law includes exceptions. District guidance describes exceptions for students with an Individualized Education Program (IEP) or Section 504 plan that requires a device, and for documented medical needs. The law also requires discipline for violations, and districts describe confiscating devices and returning them to a parent or guardian.

## Why does it matter to students?

The rule applies to students in every Texas public school, including across the Houston area, and it affects the daily routine. Several districts, including Comal ISD and Pflugerville ISD, apply it from the first bell to dismissal, which includes lunch and passing periods. Devices must be off and stored, not just silenced.

Details are left to districts. Storage methods, warnings, and penalties are set in each district's student code of conduct, so the rules can look different from one school to the next.

## What supporters say

The Senate Research Center's analysis presents the author's case: research suggests phone use may contribute to a mental health crisis among young people and affect attention spans and social development. The analysis points to similar laws in other states, including Florida, Indiana, Ohio, South Carolina, and Louisiana, and cites Pew Research Center findings that a majority of U.S. adults support banning phone use by middle and high school students during classes.

Districts describe their own goals in similar terms: fewer distractions, more face-to-face interaction, and a more focused classroom.

## What has happened so far?

Gov. Greg Abbott signed the bill in June 2025. The law's effective date was September 1, 2025, but many districts began enforcing their policies on the first day of the 2025-26 school year in August.
