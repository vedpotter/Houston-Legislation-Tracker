## What is this?

House Bill 1481, or HB 1481, is a Texas law passed in 2025. It requires every public school district and open-enrollment charter school in the state to adopt a written policy that prohibits students from using personal communication devices on school property during the school day. It changed Section 37.082 of the Texas Education Code.

Before HB 1481, whether to limit phones was left to each district. Now every district must have a policy.

## Why does it matter?

The law applies to every Texas public school, including those across the Houston area. For most students, it changes something they deal with daily: where their phone is between the first bell and the last.

## What does the law actually say?

- **A required policy.** Every district and open-enrollment charter school must adopt a written policy prohibiting student use of personal communication devices during the school day.
- **A broad list of devices.** District guidance describes covered devices as including phones, tablets, smartwatches, earbuds, and other devices capable of digital communication. School-issued devices used for instruction are not covered.
- **Exceptions.** District guidance describes exceptions for students whose IEP or Section 504 plan requires a device, and for documented medical needs.
- **Consequences.** The law requires discipline for violations. Districts describe confiscating devices and returning them to a parent or guardian.

## How could this affect students?

- **Bell to bell in many districts.** Several districts apply the rule from the first bell to dismissal, including lunch and passing periods, and require devices to be off and stored away.
- **Rules differ locally.** Districts decide the details of storage, enforcement, and penalties in their student codes of conduct, so what happens at your school depends on your district's policy.
- **Communication with families.** Reporting on the law notes that phones are how many parents stay in touch with their children during the day, which is one reason the details of each district's policy and exceptions matter.
- **What is not yet clear.** Effects on learning, attendance, and discipline are still being studied, and this tracker has not yet found Texas-specific results.
